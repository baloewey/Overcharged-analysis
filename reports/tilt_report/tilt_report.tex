\begin{abstract}
The wall $+\half$ defects of an overcharged active cavity tilt from the wall normal. At leading order the tilt follows the flow-alignment law $\tau=\arccos(1/\lambda)$, $60^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$. With 10 seeds we find that this holds only for sparse wall layers. The tilt falls steadily as the wall defects crowd together, from $\approx64^\circ$ at spacings above 20 cells to $\approx42^\circ$ at 7 cells. The fall collapses on the wall-defect spacing. It appears in circles, ellipses and Cassini ovals, and it also appears in passive layers driven by an imposed vortex. We tested the candidate explanations in turn:
\begin{itemize}
\item measurement artefacts;
\item a weaker mean wall shear;
\item the defects' own active flow;
\item the finite-shear correction to the tilt law;
\item static elasticity, at a flat wall and in the circle;
\item the bulk spiral;
\item the continuum Q-tensor model in flat and circular geometries.
\end{itemize}
Most of these fail or explain only part of the drop. The 16 October version identified an elastic pull towards the untilted registry, from
splay $\neq$ bend, as the leading candidate. That candidate is withdrawn: the splay--bend anisotropy of MPCD is produced by the director--flow coupling
in the noise, and the static response is isotropic, $K\approx44\,\kT$ (reports/frank\_findings). With one Frank constant the layer has no static
preference for any registry; the flow fixes the tilt and the tilt--position lock fixes the positions, and we verified that the positions follow the
tilt as it drops (slope 1.00 over 1454 runs). The near-wall torque assumed by the boundary-layer correction fails three direct tests in MPCD and in the
continuum (Test 6). What remains established is the measured drop and its ordering by depth over alignment length. Run by run, about half of the drop (two-thirds in
passive layers) is the near-wall field rotating less and the rest is a shrinking excess carried by each defect's neighbourhood; both follow the depth of
the cores, and the depth follows the one-constant elastic equilibrium of the ring (images and neighbours). A theory of the two depth-dependent parts is still missing. The last sections list
what the paper can safely claim and the open questions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background and definitions}

\paragraph{Tilt law.} Relative to the wall normal, the anchored director turns by $(k-1)\phi$. A wall $+\half$ at azimuth $\phi_j$ therefore has its tilt locked to its position:
\begin{equation}
\tau_j=(2k-2)\phi_j+c .\label{eq:lock}
\end{equation}
A common tilt of the layer is a rigid shift of its registry. In shear next to a no-slip wall the far-field director settles at the Leslie angle $\theta_L$, with $\cos2\theta_L=1/\lambda$. The layer slides along this zero mode until the far field is aligned, so $\tau=2\theta_L=\arccos(1/\lambda)$. The master document adds a finite-shear correction from the torque passed through the band between wall and core:
\begin{equation}
\tau=\arccos\frac1\lambda+d\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_1 s}{K}}\,(\lambda^2-1)^{-1/4},\label{eq:corr}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the core depth and $s$ the wall shear rate. The correction is always positive. In MPCD the Jeffery rotation is scaled by the shear
susceptibility $\chi_\mathrm{HI}=0.5$, so $\gamma_1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} and in $\ell_E$ is to be read as $\chi_\mathrm{HI}\gamma_1$ (corrected 22 October). Frank constant:
$K\approx44\,\kT$, isotropic in the static response (the 42/23 split measured before is a fluctuation property of the flow-coupled algorithm).

\paragraph{Definitions used throughout.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tilt.} $\tau=\psi-\theta_n$, the angle between the radial arm of the $+\half$ comet (the defect angle $\psi$ in \texttt{defects.dat}) and the outward wall normal. We checked analytically that the MPCD defect angle, the flat-wall direction of $\nabla\!\cdot\!Q$ and the ``head'' of the analytic calculations are all this same direction.
\item \textbf{Coherence.} $C=|\langle e^{i\tau_j}\rangle|$ over the wall defects of one frame (wall = within 4 cells of the wall).
\item \textbf{Tilt of the coherent layer.} The median over frames with $C>0.9$ of $|\arg\langle e^{i\tau_j}\rangle|$.
\item \textbf{Spacing.} $a=2\pi R/(2k-2)$ in circles, and the local site spacing $a(s)=\pi/(2\pi k/P-\kappa(s))$ in elongated cavities.
\item \textbf{Wall density.} $\rho=k/\pi R$.
\item \textbf{Alignment length.} $\ell_E=\sqrt{K/(\chi_\mathrm{HI}\gamma_1 s\sqrt{\lambda^2-1})}$, the thickness of the shear boundary layer over which the director turns from the anchoring to $\theta_L$. With $K=44$, $\gamma_1=60$, $\chi_\mathrm{HI}=0.5$: 2.3--4.4 cells in MPCD (the 16 October version gave 1.4--2.7, with $K=32.5$ and without $\chi_\mathrm{HI}$).
\end{itemize}

\section{The observation}

(b) Elongated cavities: local tilt of locally coherent triplets of wall defects against the local site spacing (run medians, 3 seeds). (c) Passive layers under an imposed vortex (two amplitudes) and active layers at the same radius $R=1.43\,\la$.}\label{fig:obs}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The drop.} The tilt falls from $64.6^\circ$ ($\rho\approx0.04$) through 63.0, 59.2, 54.7 and 49.4 to $44.2^\circ$ ($\rho\approx0.14$). These are 506 runs with 10 seeds each (Fig.~\ref{fig:obs}a).
\item \textbf{Collapse on spacing.} A quadratic fit in $\log a$ leaves $1.7^\circ$ rms, against a raw spread of $7.0^\circ$. Fits in $k$ or $R$ alone leave $5$--$6^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:heat}). The tilt sits at $\approx64^\circ$ for $a\gtrsim20$ cells, crosses $60^\circ$ at $a\approx15$ and reaches $\approx42^\circ$ at $a=7$. A phenomenological form $\tau=\tau_L/(1+(a^*/a)^2)$ fits with $\tau_L=67.4^\circ$, $a^*=5.35$ cells and $2.0^\circ$ rms; it is a description, not a derivation.
\item \textbf{Elongated cavities} (Fig.~\ref{fig:obs}b). Measured from the local normal (the lock holds there too: resultant 0.94--0.98), ellipses and ovals show the same fall. Their sparse layers tilt more than circles, at $66$--$71^\circ$.
\item \textbf{Passive layers} (Fig.~\ref{fig:obs}c). Under an imposed vortex the tilt falls just as steeply, $69^\circ\to54^\circ$ between $a=20$ and $7.7$ at swirl 0.03. A stronger swirl raises the tilt at fixed spacing ($k=6$: $59.5^\circ$ at 0.01, $69^\circ$ at 0.03). Activity is therefore not required for the drop.
\end{itemize}

\section{Test 1: is it a measurement effect? \verdict{red}{Mostly no}}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig2_measurement}
\caption{(a) Estimators of the tilt against wall density. The earlier table value signed by the circulation (3 seeds) overstates the drop at high $\rho$; the coherent-frame tilt does not. (b) Orientation offset $c$ of the cores from the lock, signed with the tilt, and the tilt itself, against spacing.}\label{fig:meas}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Estimators.} The first report of the drop ($33$--$43^\circ$ at $\rho\ge0.12$) used the frame-mean tilt signed by the circulation and sampled every 100 t.u. Crowded layers scatter more and switch circulation more often, which pulls that average down. In addition, the column named \texttt{tilt\_coh} in that table is the magnitude of the mean angle, not the coherence. Restricting to frames in which the layer clearly shares one tilt ($C>0.9$) removes both effects. The drop survives, at a smaller size: $44^\circ$ instead of $38^\circ$ at $\rho\approx0.14$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:meas}a).

\paragraph{Are the cores relaxed?} Your point was that the logarithmic interaction assumes each defect's orientation has relaxed to its minimum. Unrelaxed orientations exert torques. We measured the offset $c$ of each frame's cores from the lock, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lock}. Sparse layers are relaxed ($c=-0.4^\circ$). Crowded layers are twisted back toward the normal by $5$--$6^\circ$, and the resultant falls from 0.99 to 0.97 (Fig.~\ref{fig:meas}b). This is real, but it accounts for only $\approx5^\circ$ of the $\approx20^\circ$ drop. The rest is the registry: crowded defects sit closer to their homeotropic sites.

\paragraph{The positions follow the tilt (new, 22 Oct; theory/lockshift).} If the flow sets the tilt and the lock ties position to orientation, the
shift of the wall defects from the homeotropic points must fall with the tilt. Measured as arg $Z_{2k-2}$ ($=(2k-2)\times$ the angular shift; frames with exactly
$2k-2$ coherent wall defects), against the tilt, over 1454 active runs (spacing 50 to 4 cells): slope 1.00; spacing $<8$: tilt $44.0^\circ$, shift $49.6^\circ$;
8--12: 57.0/62.1; 12--20: 61.6/65.5; $>20$: 64.4/64.9. Passive imposed-vortex layers and $\lambda$ scans at fixed crowding ($k=6$, 14; tilt 47--$78^\circ$) lie on the same
line. A shift frozen at its sparse value is ruled out. The small positive offset (shift ahead of the tilt by $4$--$5^\circ$, $\approx0.1$ cell, for spacing below 20)
is the core twist of the previous paragraph seen from the positions.

\paragraph{Tracking errors of that offset (new, 22 Oct; theory/trackerr).} Statistical: bootstrap standard error of the bin medians 0.1--$0.25^\circ$.
Systematic, tested two ways. Seeded layers with known truth (first frames of runs/rest\_pinning): error of (shift $-$ tilt) $+3.5\pm4.0$, $+0.4\pm2.2$,
$-0.4\pm2.2$, $-2.5\pm1.4^\circ$ at spacing 32, 19, 11, 6; positions unbiased within 0.1 cell. Real frames (39 runs): sub-cell positions from the
order-parameter field change the offset by less than $1^\circ$, and the tilt from $\nabla\!\cdot\!Q$ restores it. Tracking accounts for at most 1--$2^\circ$, with no
consistent sign; the offset is real.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../master/theory/lockshift/fig_lock_shift}
\caption{(a) Shift of the wall defects from the homeotropic points (times $2k-2$) against the tilt, one point per run; dashed: shift follows the tilt;
dotted: shift frozen at the sparse value. (b) Deviation against spacing.}\label{fig:shift}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Is the angle measured the same way everywhere? Yes.} All analyses measure the angle between the radial arm of the comet and the outward normal:
\begin{itemize}
\item MPCD uses the tracker angle (Vromans--Giomi, the direction of $\nabla\!\cdot\!Q$);
\item the continuum codes use $\nabla\!\cdot\!Q$ directly;
\item the analytic calculations use $2\theta_{\rm rest}$, which is the same direction.
\end{itemize}
On seeded rings with known geometry the tracker recovers the intended 0, 60 and 120$^\circ$ to within 1--2$^\circ$ in median for spacings $\ge11$ cells. On about 5000 coherent director frames at $R=31.8$, the tracker tilt and the tilt from $\nabla\!\cdot\!Q$ of the MPCD director field agree at every $k$, with a median difference $\le0.8^\circ$. Both show the drop ($64.5\to48^\circ$). Tracker positions show no offset against seeded positions.

\section{Test 2: is it the flow? \verdict{orange}{Partly}}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig3_flow}
\caption{(a) Interior circulation and strain/rotation ratio in the wall band at fixed $R=1.43\,\la$: neither changes with spacing. (b) Rotation of the principal strain axes two cells inside the layer, in each wall defect's own frame: active layers (10-seed window) and passive layers. (c) Leslie angle of that local flow against the measured tilt.}\label{fig:flow}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Mean wall shear: \verdict{red}{fails}.} Your first idea was that the layer's self-flow opposes the circulation, so a denser layer weakens the wall shear. At fixed $R$, however, the azimuthally averaged wall-band shear is the same for every $k$ (strain $\approx0.09$--$0.10$, strain/rotation 1.10--1.15), and so is the interior circulation. The tilt law also predicts no dependence on the shear magnitude, only on the strain/rotation ratio, and that ratio is unchanged.

\paragraph{Local flow at the layer: \verdict{orange}{works for active, fails for passive}.} The defects' flow lobes repeat with period $a$ and cancel in the azimuthal mean, so we sampled the flow in each wall defect's frame.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Active layers.} Two cells inside the layer the strain axes rotate steadily, from $-17^\circ$ (sparse) through 0 at $a\approx13$ to $+11^\circ$ (crowded). The Leslie angle of that local flow tracks the measured tilt bin by bin: $49.6/52.0/57.0/65.1^\circ$ against $45.8/51.2/60.8/64.4^\circ$ for $a$ = 5--8/8--12/12--20/20--60 (Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}b,c).
\item \textbf{Passive layers.} The local flow is plain wall shear at every spacing: strain-axis rotation $0\pm1^\circ$, local Leslie angle $63$--$67^\circ$. The tilt still falls.
\item \textbf{The active core.} It carries the defect's own extensional self-flow: $e_{xx}\approx-0.02$, strain axes rotated by $-21$ to $-27^\circ$ at the core. Along the wall this flow runs against the circulation in sparse layers.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Reading:} the local-flow change in active layers is the active self-flow and goes with the tilt. It is not needed for the drop, because passive layers fall equally with an unchanged local flow. In active layers it is most likely a consequence of the crowded, less tilted layer rather than its cause.

\section{Test 3: elasticity and site selection \verdict{red}{No static pull (revised 22 Oct)}}

\paragraph{What changed.} The 16 October version of this test rested on $K_1\approx42>K_3\approx23\,\kT$. New measurements (reports/frank\_findings) show
that this anisotropy is a property of the noise: with the director--flow coupling off the fluctuation constants are equal ($43.8\,\kT$), with it on they
follow $\lambda$ ($K_1/K_3=0.49$--1.97 for $\lambda=-2$ to 2), and the static response to a field is isotropic at every scale tested ($K_1/K_3\approx0.81\pm0.07$
with the coupling, $0.96\pm0.05$ without). The defects respond to an essentially isotropic $K\approx44\,\kT$. Everything below the line ``superseded'' in the
old version used $K_1\neq K_3$; it is kept in the figures only for the record.

\paragraph{What holds with one Frank constant.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The lock.} $\tau_j=(2k-2)\phi_j+c$ holds with resultant length 0.99; sliding a defect along the wall rotates it with the anchoring.
\item \textbf{No static preference for any registry.} With one constant the lock is a zero mode: a common shift of the layer together with the
matching rotation costs nothing. The ``elastic minimum at the untilted layer'', the first-order $\cos\tau$ potential, the bulk-vortex term, the barrier of
80--110\,$\kT$ and the second harmonic $A_2$ (``elasticity alone gives $35^\circ$'') all came from $K_1\neq K_3$ and are withdrawn.
\item \textbf{The lock valley} (theory/lockvalley). Treating orientation as a slow variable (rigid cores of prescribed orientation, field relaxed outside;
circle $R=31.8$, ring of $2k-2$ plus a central $+1$, $K=44$): flat along the lock to within 1--1.5\,$\kT$ per defect (grid), stiff across it, with twist
stiffness 24--28\,$\kT$/rad$^2$ per defect for sparse layers, 18 at $k=10$, 12 at $k=16$. Moving a defect at \emph{fixed tilt} by half a spacing costs
12.5, 15.9, 10.0 and 7.1\,$\kT$ at $k=4$, 6, 10, 16: once the flow fixes the tilt, the anchoring sites are minima for the position. The softening with
crowding follows spacing/depth within $\pm20\%$; the numbers are uncertain by up to $2\times$ with the core size.
\item \textbf{Site selection by the flow.} The active registry ends at the same place for every initial condition because the flow fixes the tilt and
the lock fixes the positions. The one-constant continuum does the same (registry offset 0.01--0.24\,rad against 0.03 in MPCD), and passive layers switched
to active all end at that registry from eight different starting registries (runs/switchon).
\item \textbf{The passive registry is not pinned}: a free zero mode, consistent with the resting-state runs, in which seeded tilts of 60 and $120^\circ$ are kept for
100--300\,t.u.\ (an 80--110\,$\kT$ barrier would have pulled them back to $0^\circ$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Consequence for the crowding drop.} With isotropic elasticity there is no static pull towards the untilted registry, at a flat wall or in the
circle. The continuum disks with $K_1/K_3=0.55$ reproduced the drop only because they had such a pull; they are not a model of MPCD, and neither is the 1.82
model. The tilt below $60^\circ$ in crowded layers must therefore come from the flow side: what the near-wall field aligns to, and how firmly the lock is
held (Tests 4 and 6). The static twist stiffness softening with crowding (item 3) is the elastic ingredient that remains.

\section{Test 4: depth and the alignment length \verdict{orange}{Orders the data; depth chain identified, not quantitative}}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig5_depth}
\caption{(a) Core depth below the wall against spacing, active and passive. (b) Tilt against $d\sqrt{s}$ with linear fits. (c) Tilt minus $\arccos(1/\lambda)$: the correction term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} can only be positive.}\label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What fits.} Crowded cores sit closer to the wall: median depth $1.7\to1.2$ cells (active) and $1.9\to1.3$ (passive) between sparse and crowded layers. The tilt is linear in $d\sqrt{s}$ for both sets:
\begin{itemize}
\item passive: $\tau\approx20^\circ+107\,d\sqrt{s}$, with $2.3^\circ$ rms (raw $5.6^\circ$);
\item active: $\tau\approx24^\circ+59\,d\sqrt{s}$, with $3.0^\circ$ rms (raw $6.7^\circ$).
\end{itemize}
Both fits beat spacing alone ($3.4^\circ$/$3.5^\circ$) and depth alone ($4.2^\circ$/$4.3^\circ$). The shear rate matters at fixed depth: passive $k=8$ at $d=1.46$ gives $55.5^\circ$ at $s=0.043$ and $63^\circ$ at $s=0.072$. Since $d\sqrt{s}\propto d/\ell_E$, the variable that orders the data is the core depth measured in units of the shear boundary layer. With the corrected
$\ell_E$ (2.3--4.4 cells) the MPCD cores sit at $d/\ell_E\approx0.3$--0.7; the ordering is unchanged (a uniform rescaling).

\paragraph{Independent test: shear at fixed spacing (new, 19 Oct).} Passive circles under an imposed vortex at fixed $k$ (6: spacing $\approx19$; 13: $\approx7.8$), with the
forcing varied tenfold. The line fitted above on the crowding runs predicts these runs without refitting (Table~\ref{tab:shearscan}): at $k=6$ the tilt rises from
$54.6^\circ$ to $66.9^\circ$ as $d\sqrt{s}$ grows, with residuals $-0.4$ to $+2.1^\circ$; at $k=13$ the cores sit shallower ($d\approx1.3$ against 1.7--2.0), so at the
same wall shear ($s\approx0.063$) the tilt is $12$--$15^\circ$ lower, again on the line (residuals $-3.1$, $-0.8^\circ$). Spacing and shear act only through $d\sqrt{s}$ in this range.
The line overshoots at the strongest forcing ($k=6$: $68.5^\circ$ against $73^\circ$; $k=13$, $A=0.1$: $63^\circ$ against $73^\circ$, few coherent frames), where the tilt saturates.

\paragraph{The drop shrinks with flow alignment (new, 19 Oct).} Active circles of the same size (area 2500) with a sparse ($k=6$, spacing $\approx17$) and a
crowded ($k=14$, spacing $\approx6.8$) layer, $\lambda=2$--6. Coherent-layer tilts, sparse/crowded: $62.5/47.6^\circ$ ($\lambda=2$), $71.9/65.7^\circ$ (3),
$74.9/71.2^\circ$ (4), $78.0/77.4^\circ$ (6). The crowding drop falls from $15^\circ$ to $6$, $4$ and $<1^\circ$. Both layers keep their full $2k-2$ and bulk $+1$ at every $\lambda$.
Stronger alignment overrides whatever pins the crowded layer, as a flow-versus-pinning balance with a drive growing with $\lambda$ would predict; this is
a constraint for any quantitative model (source: master/theory/robust/robust\_sets.txt).

\paragraph{Why the correction term itself fails.} Its assumed mechanism fails directly (Test 6): the band is not rotated forward. In addition, Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} has the right trend on the sparse side: as $d$ shrinks the positive excess shrinks, which explains a fall from 64--69$^\circ$ toward $60^\circ$. But the term is positive by construction, so it cannot produce tilts below $60^\circ$, and crowded layers sit at 44--55$^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:depth}c). Fitted with its leading term fixed at $60^\circ$, it leaves residuals that still correlate with spacing ($+0.8$). The fitted intercepts of 20--24$^\circ$ say that, in the data, the tilt is not ``$60^\circ$ plus a correction'' at all.

\paragraph{The director across the band (Fig.~\ref{fig:pot}c).} In MPCD the director angle from the tangent peaks right inside the layer, at 25--28$^\circ$ (sparse) and 22--25$^\circ$ (crowded). It never reaches $\theta_L=30^\circ$, and it decays to zero by 9--10 cells, where the wall-sign shear ends. The layer tilt exceeds twice that peak by $15^\circ$ (k\,3), $9^\circ$ (k\,6), $5^\circ$ (k\,10) and $3$--$4^\circ$ (k\,14--20). So two things fall together with crowding: the defects' over-rotation past the local field (which behaves like the positive finite-depth correction), and the local director itself. That twice the peak angle is 45--55$^\circ$ for every $k$ suggests that the sparse-layer agreement with $\arccos(1/\lambda)$ is partly a compensation: incomplete alignment in a thin shear zone, plus over-rotation of deep cores. The run-by-run version of this comparison follows.

\paragraph{Lock to the local field, run by run (new, 22 Oct; theory/locallock; Fig.~\ref{fig:locallock}).} If the layer simply locked to the field
next to it, the tilt would be twice the director angle just inside the layer. Per run we measured $\alpha_1$, the ring-averaged director angle from the
local tangent one cell inside the wall defects (estimator of Fig.~\ref{fig:pot}c, signed by the circulation), and split the tilt into the local field and
an excess, $\tau=2\alpha_1+(\tau-2\alpha_1)$. 41 active runs (main sweep seeds 1--3 and the switch-on reruns) and 21 passive imposed-vortex runs:

two-thirds of the passive one (8 of 12$^\circ$, with the peak field) is the local field rotating less; the rest is the excess shrinking. Both parts
are depth effects. The excess is the rotated neighbourhood of Test 6, and it grows with depth (correlation with log spacing $+0.84$--0.87). The local
field is sampled closer to the anchored wall when the cores are shallower. The core twist back towards the normal (Test 1, $\approx5^\circ$) is part of the
excess.

\paragraph{What sets the depth (new, 22 Oct).} The one-constant equilibrium radius of a ring of $2k-2$ wall $+\half$ with a central $+1$ in a disk
(images and neighbours; the master depth law) predicts the measured depth with correlation 0.94 (active) and 0.93 (passive). In crowded walls it
matches almost exactly (1.13 vs 1.17; passive 1.29 vs 1.28). Sparse defects sit shallower than this elastic equilibrium (active 1.72 vs 2.35), so
the flow pushes sparse cores towards the wall. So the chain is: crowding $\to$ a shallower elastic equilibrium depth (one-constant images and
neighbours) $\to$ cores in a less-rotated part of the near-wall field with a smaller rotated neighbourhood $\to$ a lower tilt. This uses only
one-constant elasticity. What it lacks is a theory of the two depth-dependent parts (the near-wall field and the excess).

\paragraph{The same split in the continuum (new, 22 Oct; shear\_tilt/continuum\_local\_lock.txt).} The active one-constant continuum disk also has the
drop (Test 5), and splitting its tilt the same way: at $\zeta=3$, $k=4$ (sparse) $65.0^\circ=51.3+13.7$ (depth 1.33), $k=16$ (crowded) $58.0^\circ=55.6+2.5$ (depth
0.39); the same holds at $\zeta=3$--8 and with an added imposed swirl. Sparse layers split almost exactly as in MPCD ($50.4+13.4$): the continuum
near-wall field is not fully aligned either (2$\alpha\approx51^\circ$, also deeper in). In the continuum the drop is carried entirely by the excess, which
shrinks as the cores approach the wall (its local field even rises slightly); in MPCD both parts fall. The common part of the mechanism is therefore
the shrinking rotated neighbourhood of shallower cores; MPCD adds a lower near-wall field in crowded walls (the continuum crowded cores sit at
0.3--0.4 cells, below the grid spacing, so that part may not be comparable). The passive continuum disk under an imposed vortex falls only to
$\arccos(1/\lambda)$ ($68\to62^\circ$), and the passive flat wall's far field is exactly flow-aligned (Test 6); its fall is the excess alone.

\section{Test 5: continuum models \verdict{orange}{Passive: no drop; active one-constant: a drop}}

\emph{Revised reading (22 Oct).} MPCD's static elasticity is one-constant, so the one-constant runs are the matching ones; the $K_1/K_3=1.82$ and 0.55
runs are kept for the record. The active one-constant disk, which shows a crowding drop of 8--$11^\circ$ without any anisotropy, is now the closest
continuum model of MPCD's drop.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Passive disk, one constant or $K_1/K_3=1.82$:} mild fall from $\approx68^\circ$ to $62^\circ$ toward $\arccos(1/\lambda)$, never below it.
\item \textbf{Passive disk, $K_1/K_3=0.55$:} $63.6\to54.7^\circ$, below $60^\circ$ at spacing $\lesssim15$. This is a smaller drop than MPCD's.
\item \textbf{Active disk} (one constant, backflow, $\zeta=0.3$ and 1): the self-organised overcharged state appears at every $k$, and the tilt stays at 60.5--64$^\circ$. There is no crowding drop.
\item \textbf{Flat wall with finite anchoring} (Rapini--Papoular): with the layer kept ($W=1$) the tilt is 65--71$^\circ$; for weaker anchoring the defect is absorbed into the wall. MPCD's anchoring is in fact strong: the wall-cell director deviates by only 2.4--5$^\circ$ between defects at every spacing. What drops with crowding is the order at the wall ($0.91\to0.79$; $0.65$ at cores).
\item \textbf{Active disk with noise or stronger activity} (new, 19 Oct; one constant, backflow, $k=4$ and 16). Additive noise on $Q$ ($D=0.002$, 0.01) leaves the tilt
at $61.4$--$61.5^\circ$ for both $k$: noise alone gives no drop. At $\zeta=3$ the tilt is $65.2^\circ$ ($k=4$) against $57.3^\circ$ ($k=16$): an $8^\circ$ crowding drop
appears with activity. The $\zeta=3$ disk stays laminar (coherence 1.00, steady tilt, two bulk $+\half$, no pairs) with a fully ordered bulk spiral (order 1.1), so this drop needs neither turbulence nor the loss of the spiral; noise disorders the spiral (order 0.7) without a drop. Its cores sit at $d/\ell_E=1.28$ ($k=4$) and 0.42 ($k=16$); with the corrected MPCD range (0.3--0.7) the continuum no longer brackets MPCD (MPCD sparse layers sit near the continuum's crowded value); the crowded value is limited by resolution (core depth 0.39 cells, grid 0.5).
\item \textbf{Activity scan, $\zeta=3$--10} (new, 20 Oct). Both disks keep a single vortex up to $\zeta=8$; the crowding drop is $7.9^\circ$ ($\zeta=3$), 7.7 and $8.1^\circ$ ($\zeta=4$, 5), then 9.4, 10.2 and $10.7^\circ$ ($\zeta=6$--8). A few bulk $-\half$ appear from $\zeta=4$; sustained pair creation starts at $\zeta=7$ (sparse) and 8 (crowded); at $\zeta=8$ the sparse disk's spiral breaks up (order 0.19), the crowded one stays ordered (1.17); at $\zeta=10$ the crowded disk loses its vortex. $d/\ell_E$ stays at 1.2--1.7 (sparse) and 0.42--0.53 (crowded, resolution-limited), against 0.3--0.7 in MPCD (corrected). The scan cannot separate turbulence from the growing wall shear: the largest step comes before sustained pair creation. The continuum drop is about half of the MPCD drop at the same radius ($14^\circ$) at $\zeta=3$--5 and about three-quarters at $\zeta=8$.
\item \textbf{MPCD at twice the density} (thermal-noise amplitude down by $\sqrt2$; $R=31.8$, 2 seeds): the crowded tilt of coherent frames is unchanged ($k=16$: $50.4^\circ$ at density 40 and 20), although at density 40 the layer is coherent in only 27\% of frames (74\% at density 20) and the flow is faster. The sparse $k=4$ circle leaks at density 40 (4.1 of 6 wall defects), probably through a change of the active length, so it is not a clean comparison.
\item \textbf{Flat wall with a melted wall} (strong director anchoring, $S_w=0.6$ or 0.4): little change for one constant or 1.82; with 0.55 the drop deepens slightly at intermediate spacing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Test 6: the near-wall torque \verdict{red}{Fails as assumed (new, 22 Oct)}}

\paragraph{What was tested.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} assumes that in the band between the wall and the defects the anchoring holds the director, so only
the vorticity acts, with a torque $\chi\gamma_1 s d/2$ per unit length passed to the layer. Three predictions follow: an overtilt of $\approx14^\circ$ in MPCD
(measured 2--$4^\circ$ in sparse active layers); a forward rotation of the band, $\chi\gamma_1sd^2/(4K)=+1$ to $+4^\circ$; and a spring on the common position of the
layer, $k_D=K\pi^2/(a\ell_E)$ per defect.

\paragraph{Band rotation} (theory/bandrot; Fig.~\ref{fig:band}). For each frame: the measured cell director minus the exact static one-constant field of
the same tracked defects (all defects with images, anchoring $\theta=k\phi$ exact), cells more than 1.5 cells from any core, against distance $u$ from the
wall, signed so that positive is further in the sense of the tilt. Without flow this is zero. MPCD passive imposed vortex (18 runs): about 0 at the wall,
$-1$ to $-2^\circ$ at $u=0.75$, $-4$ to $-8^\circ$ at the layer, $-10$ to $-20^\circ$ at $u=7$ (wall shear 0.04--0.08). MPCD active ($k=6$ switch-on reruns, director every
10\,t.u.): $-1.8$, $-7^\circ$, $-21^\circ$. Continuum flat wall (Q tensor minus the exact static flat-wall field at the measured defect position): $-1.4$ to $-2.7^\circ$
at $u=0.75$, saturating at $-3.4$ to $-5.4^\circ$, which equals $-(\tau/2-30^\circ)$: the far field is exactly flow-aligned and the overtilted defect is ahead of it.
\emph{Reading:} the band is not rotated forward in either model; the overtilt is carried by the defect and its neighbourhood. (In MPCD the deep values also
contain the flow-aligned interior, which the static reference lacks; only the near-wall sign and slope are clean.)

\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../master/theory/bandrot/fig_band_rotation}
\caption{MPCD passive layers under an imposed vortex: flow-induced director rotation against distance from the wall, all azimuths (a) and
between/at the wall defects (b). Grey: band between wall and defects. The band estimate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} is $+1$ to $+4^\circ$.}\label{fig:band}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Core rotation relative to its surroundings} (theory/bandrot/core\_rotation). Core orientation from $\nabla\!\cdot\!Q$ within 1.6 cells; ring
orientation from the director at 2--3 and 3--4.5 cells around the defect; measured minus static, signed by the tilt. No-flow controls: MPCD seeded layers
$0\pm1^\circ$ (ring 2--3), $0\pm2^\circ$ (3--4.5); continuum at rest biased when the core is within $\approx1.5$ cells of the wall ($-9$ to $-23^\circ$), $\approx0$ for depth
$\ge2$. MPCD passive: the core leads the 2--3 cell ring by $+1.3$ to $+5^\circ$ (median $\approx2.5^\circ$; $+5^\circ$ at the strongest shear), the 3--4.5 cell ring by $+5$ to
$+14^\circ$; active $+3^\circ$ and $+9$--$10^\circ$. Continuum at clean depth (spacing 40--60, $s=0.01$, depth 2.0--2.4): $-1.5$ to $+1.4^\circ$ although the overtilt
there is $+9$--$12^\circ$. \emph{Reading:} the continuum core does not over-rotate relative to its surroundings; its whole neighbourhood is rotated and relaxes to
the far field beyond 4.5 cells ($\ell_E\approx7.6$ cells there). In MPCD the core leads its immediate surroundings by about the size of the overtilt and the
lead grows over a few cells, consistent with MPCD's shorter $\ell_E$.

\paragraph{Core size.} Rigid-core radius 1.0/1.5/2.5 cells in the Frank model: twist stiffness 19/28/25 ($k=6$) and 13/12/7\,$\kT$/rad$^2$ ($k=16$); continuum
core length 0.5/1/2 cells at fixed $K$: overtilt $+4.4/+5.3/+6.4^\circ$ (spacing 8) and $+8.3/+8.3/+7.1^\circ$ (spacing 20), mostly through the depth. The melted
core is not the cause of the overtilt.

\paragraph{Stiffness of the selected positions} (theory/regstiff). Collective position fluctuations of the wall layer against the spring $k_D$:
the effective temperature needed, $T_\mathrm{eff}=(\text{measured}/\text{predicted at }\kT)^2$, is 6.5 (median; 153 active runs) and 5.7 in passive imposed-vortex
layers (22 runs), where the noise is thermal and $\approx1$ was expected. The formula follows the spacing dependence roughly but is $\approx6\times$ too stiff.

\paragraph{Reading.} Three independent tests say the near-wall torque is much weaker than Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} assumes. The overtilt is carried by a rotated
region around each defect that relaxes to the flow-aligned far field over $\ell_E$; how large that region is relative to the depth, not core physics,
distinguishes MPCD (a few cells) from the continuum ($>4.5$ cells).

\section{Where this leaves us}

\paragraph{Established.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The drop is real, spacing-controlled, and the same in active and passive layers and in all shapes; it is ordered by $d\sqrt{s}\propto d/\ell_E$.
\item The positions of the wall defects follow their tilt through the lock as the tilt drops (slope 1.00); the flow selects the tilt, the lock the
positions. The defects respond to an isotropic Frank constant, so there is no static elastic preference for a registry.
\item The boundary-layer correction's mechanism (vorticity acting alone in an anchored band) fails; the overtilt is carried by a rotated neighbourhood
of each defect relaxing over $\ell_E$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open, and questions for Tyler.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A theory of the depth-controlled tilt.} Crowding brings the cores closer to the wall (one-constant elastic equilibrium), where the
near-wall field has rotated less and the rotated neighbourhood is smaller (Test 4). What sets the near-wall field and the excess as functions of depth,
and why does MPCD's near-wall field fall with crowding while the active continuum's does not? (In both models the excess shrinks as the cores approach the wall; the passive continuum falls only to $\arccos(1/\lambda)$.)
\item \textbf{What sets the near-wall torque?} Three tests show it is far weaker than the boundary-layer ansatz; a theory of the rotated region around
each defect (its size relative to the depth) would give both the overtilt and the stiffness of the selected positions.
\item \textbf{Is the sparse-layer agreement with $\arccos(1/\lambda)$ partly a compensation?} The local director never reaches $\theta_L$ in the thin shear zone,
and the cores lead their surroundings by 2--$3^\circ$. This bears on how strongly the paper can phrase the tilt law.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{For the paper (PRL + long SI).}
\begin{itemize}
\item State the tilt law as the sparse-layer limit: within $3^\circ$ for $\lambda=2$--6 at $\rho\approx0.045$--0.09.
\item Report the crowding drop as a measured, spacing-controlled effect ordered by $d/\ell_E$, with the mechanism open, in the SI; do not invoke an elastic
pull or splay $\neq$ bend.
\item Report site selection as flow plus lock, with the shift-follows-tilt test.
\item Quote the overcharged-circle tilt as $61$--$66^\circ$ at low wall density.
\item Calculations that assume a $63^\circ$ layer (binding, shielding) apply to sparse windings only.
\item None of the main claims (overcharging, flow-induced ejection, leaking and the wall law) depends on the tilt value. They rely on a common tilt, not on its size.
\end{itemize}
