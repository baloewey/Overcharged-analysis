\section{Simulation method and parameters}\label{si:methods}
We simulate a two-dimensional active nematic with the nematic multi-particle collision dynamics solver NIAMH-MPCD \cite{Shendruk2015,Kozhukhov2022,Kozhukhov2024};
we use the method as published and summarise only what enters this work. Units: cell size $a=1$, particle mass $m=1$, $\kT=1$, so the time unit is
$\tau_0=a\sqrt{m/\kT}$ (written t.u.); time step $\delta t=0.1$; mean density 20 particles per cell.

\paragraph{Model.} Particles stream ballistically and then collide within square cells whose grid is shifted randomly every step. The velocity collision
(operator 21) is the Andersen-thermostatted, angular-momentum-conserving rule plus an active force dipole: every particle in a cell receives a kick
$\pm\delta t\,f(N_c)\sum_{j\in c}\zeta_j\,\hat{\mathbf n}_c$ along the cell director, of opposite sign on either side of a plane through the cell's centre
of mass, with the cell average removed so that momentum is conserved (extensile for $\zeta>0$). The prefactor sums the particle activities in the cell and is
switched off smoothly in cells with more than about twice the mean occupancy, $f(N_c)=\tfrac12[1-\tanh((N_c-2\bar n)/\bar n)]$. Orientations are redrawn about
the local director from a Maier--Saupe distribution with mean-field strength $U=10\,\kT$ (local order parameter $S_c$), and rotate in the flow by Jeffery's equation
with flow-alignment (Bretherton) parameter $\lambda$ ($\lambda=2$ unless stated; $\lambda<1$ is tumbling), shear susceptibility $0.5$ and rotational friction $0.01$.
Activity $\zeta=0.07$ throughout, except for the robustness checks below.

\paragraph{Walls and anchoring.} Each cavity wall is an implicit surface. Particles crossing it are returned to the crossing point with reversed velocity
(bounce-back, no slip), and cells cut by the wall are filled with phantom particles whose velocities are drawn about zero. Anchoring is strong: every particle
in a cut cell, and every particle hitting the wall, has its orientation projected on the local anchoring axis. With the imposed winding the anchoring axis turns
by $\psi_w=2\pi k\,s/P$ along the wall (arclength $s$, perimeter $P$; on a circle $\psi_w=k\phi$), so the cavity carries total charge $k$, held by $2|k|$
defects of charge $\operatorname{sgn}(k)\,\half$ in passive cavities. For ellipses the arclength is computed from the ellipse parameter; for Cassini ovals, whose
normal angle is not monotonic along a concave neck, from the polar angle about the centre. Arclength-uniform anchoring keeps the local density of imposed defects
uniform along non-circular walls. In passive validation runs the wall director follows the imposed axis (alignment 0.96--0.99), the tracker finds exactly
$2|k|$ defects with net charge $k$ in every frame, and $-k$ runs are exact mirror images. The anchoring is imposed cell by cell and fails when neighbouring
imposed defects come closer than about 6--7 cells; all results use imposed densities $\rho=2|k|/P\le0.15$ (alignment $\approx0.90$ at 0.15, 0.93--0.97 at 0.10).

\paragraph{Cavities.} Circles of radius $R=|k|/(\pi\rho)$ for winding $k$ and imposed density $\rho$; ellipses of aspect ratio $\varepsilon$ and Cassini ovals
(curves of constant product of the distances to two foci) at fixed area; equivalent radius $R_\mathrm{eq}=\sqrt{\mathrm{area}/\pi}$. The aspect ratio of a shape is
the ratio of its overall extents along and across its long axis. Each cavity sits in the smallest box of odd side lengths that contains it, and the number of
particles is set to 20 times the cavity area. Sizes are given in units of the active length $\la$.

\paragraph{Active length.} We use $\la=22.2\pm0.3$ cells at $\zeta=0.07$, measured as $\sqrt{A/\langle N\rangle}$ with $\langle N\rangle$ the mean number of defects in a
$200\times200$ periodic box, as in Ref.~\cite{Kozhukhov2024} (stationary part chosen automatically, errors from the integrated autocorrelation time).
A $100\times100$ box gives $21.4\pm0.5$. For the activity checks the cavity sizes were scaled with $\la\propto\zeta^{-1/2}$.
%% user decision 2026-09-29: the paper does not report the l_a(zeta) scaling (published work covers it); only the calibration enters.

\paragraph{Material constants.} Frank constants from the equilibrium director fluctuations of passive, defect-free periodic boxes
($\kT A/\langle|\theta_{\mathbf q}|^2\rangle=K_1q_\perp^2+K_3q_\parallel^2$): splay $K_1=42.0\,\kT$, bend $K_3=23.0\,\kT$ ($64^2$, two seeds; $41.5$ and $23.0$ in $96^2$),
order parameter $\langle S\rangle=0.876$. Rotational viscosity $\gamma_1\approx60\,\kT\tau_0/a^2$ from the decay of director modes (splay $61.9\pm2.3$, bend $56.0\pm2.0$;
4 seeds). Mobility of free $\pm\half$ in the bulk $0.0054\,a^2/(\kT\tau_0)$, from the approach of annihilating pairs.

\paragraph{Compressibility.} MPCD is weakly compressible. In periodic boxes at $\zeta=0.07$ the particle number per cell fluctuates with standard deviation 8.1
(Poisson value 4.5) and the rms Mach number is 0.84. In cavities the circulating flow stratifies the density radially (for $k=8$ at $R=1.43\,\la$, about 25
particles per cell near the wall and 14 at the centre), and since the active force sums over the particles in a cell, the active stress follows this
stratification. This is a generic finite-Mach effect of particle-based solvers; we state it rather than remove it.

\paragraph{Imposed flows.} Passive cavities under an imposed vortex receive an azimuthal body acceleration $A$ about the cavity centre, within a disk of given radius
(production steps only). Pressure-driven channels receive a uniform body acceleration $g$ along the channel. Both are applied to every particle in the region,
before the collision step.

\paragraph{Seeded starts.} To start from prescribed defect configurations (unique-steady-state tests, Sec.~\ref{si:steady}; negative layers, Sec.~\ref{si:mirror}),
the initial director is the relaxed Frank director (anisotropic, $K_1/K_3$ as measured) of the prescribed defects with the imposed anchoring on the wall, sampled
at the cell centres and used to orient the particles at $t=0$ (no warm-up).

\paragraph{Runs and reproducibility.} Runs of one parameter set differ only by the random seed. The first three seeds of the circle sweep were made with a
build that did not honour the input seed, so they are independent but not bit-wise reproducible; all later runs reproduce byte for byte. Defects are written
every 2\,t.u., director and flow fields every 2--100\,t.u.\ depending on the set; the analysis uses the second half of each run unless stated.
Table~\ref{tab:s_runs} lists the data sets.
\paragraph{Robustness to activity.} The main results hold at $\zeta=0.06$ and 0.08 with the cavity size scaled by $\la\propto\zeta^{-1/2}$ (3 seeds each, 6000\,t.u.;
compared with $\zeta=0.07$ measured the same way). Circle, $k=6$, $R=1.43\,\la$: wall $+\half$ $9.88$, $9.97$, $9.97$ of 10; bulk charge $+1.06$, $+1.01$, $+1.01$;
coherent-layer tilt $64.1^\circ$, $63.9^\circ$, $63.5^\circ$; coherence 0.97 at all three. Aspect-2 ellipse, $R_\mathrm{eq}=1.5\,\la$, $k=7$: vacancies $4.4$, $3.8$, $3.4$, coherence 0.38--0.41,
and bulk charge per unit wall length $Q_b/P=0.0130$, 0.0128, 0.0126, within $\approx5\%$ of the wall law at the same $q$ ($\zeta=0.07$ median 0.0123).

\section{Defect detection and observables}\label{si:observables}
\paragraph{Defect detection.} Defects are found by the solver every 2\,t.u.\ from the cell directors. The charge of a cell is the winding of the director
around its eight neighbours (head--tail symmetric); cells with $\big||q|-\half\big|<0.01$ are clustered into defects of one sign, whose position is the mean of
the cells' centres. The orientation $\psi$ of a defect is the direction of $\nabla\!\cdot Q$ (centred differences of the cell $Q$ tensor), defined modulo
$2\pi$ for $+\half$ and $2\pi/3$ for $-\half$. For a $+\half$ this is the direction of its \emph{tail}; its head points along $\psi+\pi$. Bulk $+\half$ move
towards their head in 66--72\% of steps, as expected for an extensile nematic. Positions are measured from the cavity centre.

\paragraph{Charge audit.} With $N_\pm$ the numbers of defects of the sign of $k$ and of the opposite sign, a frame is consistent when
$\tfrac12\operatorname{sgn}(k)(N_+-N_-)=k$, all $2|k|$ imposed defects are present ($N_+\ge2|k|$), and the dynamical defects come in pairs. Passive validation
runs pass in every frame; active runs at $R=1.43\,\la$ in at least 98\% of frames, and the anchoring itself fails (audit below 30\%) only for $\rho\gtrsim0.25$.

\paragraph{Wall layer, wall count, vacancies, bulk charge.} The distance of a defect from the wall is measured to the nearest point of the wall polyline.
A defect of the sign of $k$ within 4 cells of the wall belongs to the wall layer (Sec.~\ref{si:layer}; in channels, moving the cut to 3 or 5 cells changes the counts by a few
tenths of a defect per wall). $N_\mathrm{wall}$ is the mean number of wall-layer defects per frame, the vacancies are $2k-2-N_\mathrm{wall}$ (positive winding; for negative
winding we report $N_\mathrm{wall}-2|k|$), and the bulk charge $Q_b$ is the mean net charge of all defects farther than 4 cells from the wall. In circles
$Q_b$ agrees with the plateau (``knee'') of the mean charge enclosed by a circle of radius $r$, which rises from a negative dip at the centre to $+1$ just inside
the layer and jumps to $k$ at the wall. The bulk charge per unit wall length, $Q_b/P$, is compared with the winding density $q=k/P$.

\paragraph{Tilt and coherence.} The tilt of a wall defect is the angle between its tail and the outward wall normal at the nearest wall point,
$\tau_j=\arg e^{i(\psi_j-\phi_{n,j})}$ for $+\half$ (for $-\half$, $\tau_j=\frac13\arg e^{3i(\psi_j-\phi_{n,j})}$, so $\tau_j=0$ means one arm into the wall).
The tilt coherence of a frame is $C=|\langle e^{i\tau_j}\rangle_j|$ over its wall-layer defects ($e^{3i\tau_j}$ for $-\half$), averaged over frames; $C=1$ when all
wall defects share one tilt. The common tilt of a frame is $\arg\langle e^{i\tau_j}\rangle_j$. We quote either the mean $|\tau_j|$ over all wall defects or the tilt of
the coherent layer, the median $|\arg\langle e^{i\tau_j}\rangle_j|$ over frames with $C>0.9$; the latter is used wherever the layer is not always coherent. Signed tilts
are signed with the circulation at the same time. The lock (Sec.~\ref{si:lock}) is tested with the concentration $|\langle e^{i(\tau_j-2\psi_\mathrm{rel})}\rangle|$
($e^{i(3\tau_j-2\psi_\mathrm{rel})}$ for $-\half$), with $\psi_\mathrm{rel}$ the anchoring angle relative to the wall normal at the defect.

\paragraph{Flow.} Flow fields are averages over the output interval (2--100\,t.u., depending on the set). Over the cells inside the cavity we compute the mean
azimuthal velocity about the centre (counter-clockwise positive), its modulus per frame $|\langle v_\phi\rangle|$, the rms speed $v_\mathrm{rms}$, the mean
tangential velocity $|v_t|$ in the band 1--5 cells from the wall, and the fraction of frames in which the circulation keeps its sign. In elongated cavities a frame
has two vortices when the two halves along the long axis rotate in opposite senses, each measured about its own centroid.

\paragraph{Escapes.} A wall-layer defect escapes when it leaves the 4-cell band and is found deeper than 6 cells within 20\,t.u.\ (nearest-neighbour links of less
than 3 cells per frame). The escape probability is the number of escapes per wall defect per 20\,t.u. A depleting escape takes a resident wall defect (in the band for the previous 40\,t.u.) out of a layer at or below
$2k-2$, without another $+\half$ entering nearby, and leaves the smoothed wall count lower for the next 10--50\,t.u. Controls are wall defects at the same time and of the same run that do not escape.

\paragraph{Averaging.} Unless stated otherwise, time averages use the second half of each run (the sweep tables drop the first 20\%), and values for a parameter set
are means over seeds with standard errors.

\paragraph{Tracker limitation.} The tracker does not report defects whose core lies in the first fluid row next to a flat wall whose inward normal points along $+y$.
In cavities this changes no count (for example $18.03$ of $18$ wall defects at $k=10$, $R_\mathrm{eq}=\la$), but cores facing the bottom of the cavity are placed up to
half a cell deeper than those facing the top. Only crowded periodic channels ($m\geq18$), which are not used here, lose defects.

\section{Anchoring geometry, the lock and pinning}\label{si:lock}
\paragraph{Discrete symmetry.} On a circle the anchored director makes the angle $(k-1)\phi$ with the local normal: homeotropic where $(k-1)\phi\equiv0$ (mod $\pi$),
tangential halfway. Rotating the cavity by $\beta$ leaves the pattern unchanged only if $(k-1)\beta\equiv0$ (mod $\pi$), so the boundary has a $(2k-2)$-fold symmetry,
with homeotropic points at $\phi_n=2\pi n/(2k-2)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_lock}a). Only $k=1$ is fully rotationally symmetric.

\paragraph{The lock.} A $+\half$ near the wall has the director field $\theta=\half\alpha+\theta_0$ around it; its radial arm (the tail) lies where $\theta=\alpha$,
and the arm pointing at the nearest wall point must carry the wall director. Hence the tail makes the angle $\tau=2\psi_\mathrm{rel}$ with the outward normal, where
$\psi_\mathrm{rel}$ is the anchoring angle relative to the normal at that point; on a circle
\begin{equation}
\tau_j=(2k-2)\phi_j+\mathrm{const}\pmod{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
The tail points straight at the wall only at a homeotropic point, and tilt and position are one degree of freedom: a common tilt $\tau$ of the ring is a rigid shift of its
registry by $\tau/(2k-2)$. On arbitrary walls $\psi_\mathrm{rel}(s)=2\pi ks/P-\phi_n(s)$, with $\phi_n(s)$ the direction of the normal at arclength $s$. For a defect of
charge $s_d$ the same argument gives $\tau=\psi_\mathrm{rel}/(1-s_d)$, i.e.\ $3\tau=2\psi_\mathrm{rel}$ for $-\half$ (Sec.~\ref{si:mirror}). The simulations obey the lock with
mean resultant length $|\langle e^{i(\tau_j-2\psi_\mathrm{rel})}\rangle|=0.99$ in $\approx150\,000$ active frames of circles ($k=3$--10; within-run slope 1.04--1.15 against 1),
and locally in every shape (0.91--0.97 in the shape sweep, 0.96 for $-\half$ in the mirror sweep).

\paragraph{Commensurability.} A ring of $2k-2$ defects can sit untilted, one defect on each homeotropic point (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_lock}b). A ring of $N$ evenly spaced defects
has consecutive tilts differing by $2(k-1)\,2\pi/N$; for $N=2k$ the step is $-2\pi/k$, so the tilts wind twice around the ring and can never all vanish or coincide
(Fig.~\ref{fig:s_lock}c). Measured in passive circles with $k=6$: $+41^\circ$, $-20^\circ$, $-91^\circ$, \dots, steps of $-60^\circ$. The ring that can take a common tilt
by a rigid shift has $2|k-1|$ defects for either sign of the winding.

\paragraph{Site pinning needs splay $\neq$ bend.} With one Frank constant a uniform rotation of the director costs nothing (rotate the defects by $\beta$ and add $(k-1)\beta$
to the director, and the boundary condition is restored), so the registry is a free sliding mode. To first order in $\Delta=(K_1-K_3)/2=9.5\,\kT$ the anisotropy pins
it. For the commensurate ring (with the bulk $+1$) the minimum lies at zero tilt, with tails at the wall, and the barrier is 80--110\,$\kT$ at $k=6$; for the passive $2k$
ring the lowest harmonics cancel and the barrier is below $1\,\kT$. Accordingly:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Active circles} end on the predicted registry from any start: six initial directors (random $\times3$, along $x$, at $45^\circ$, along $y$) and two production
seeds all reach the predicted registry (mean offset from the homeotropic points after removing the common tilt, $+0.03\pm0.07$ site spacings; mean resultant 1.00); only the sense of the vortex is chosen spontaneously.
\item With a sine-modulated winding, which displaces the homeotropic points, the active sites follow the displaced points (rms 0.21--0.27 cells, against 2.2--4.5 for an even
lattice).
\item \emph{Passive} $2k$ rings are not pinned: their registry is set by the initial condition and only creeps.
\end{itemize}
The tilted active registry is held at $\pm63^\circ$, although the elastic minimum of the commensurate ring is at zero tilt: the flow holds it there (Sec.~\ref{si:tilt}). This
site pinning is distinct from the restoring torque that sets how far the registry slides under a given flow. The static registry stiffness per defect falls with crowding,
from 19\,$\kT$/rad$^2$ at $k=3$ to 4 at $k=6$, 1.7 at $k=8$ and 0.2--0.4 at $k\geq10$ (circle $R=1.43\,\la$, where for $k\geq12$ the minimum moves to $180^\circ$), so it
cannot by itself explain why crowded layers tilt less (Sec.~\ref{si:tilt}).

\paragraph{Fluctuations and mobility.} Along the wall, the passive lattice fluctuates as predicted by the exact elastic energy of the ring with the measured bend constant

the mobility of departing defects normal to the wall is about ten times smaller (0.002).

\section{Elastic energetics of ejection}\label{si:energy}
\paragraph{Model.} We compare, in the anchored disk ($R=31.83$, $k=4$, 6, 8), two structures with the same $2k$ defects of charge $+\half$, so that core energies cancel:
(A) all $2k$ on a ring at the wall, the passive state; (B) $2k-2$ on the ring and two in the bulk at $\pm s$ on a diameter ($s=0.3$, 0.45, $0.6R$), the active state.
Each core is a rigid disk of radius $a$ (1.5 or 2.5 cells) whose director is the one-constant field of the defects plus a constant rotation. Outside the cores the director
correction (zero on the wall, so the anchoring is exact) is relaxed under the Frank energy, either one-constant ($K_1=K_3=32.5\,\kT$) or with the measured anisotropy
($K_1=42$, $K_3=23.1\,\kT$). A common tilt $\tau$ is imposed by rotating every wall core to $\tau$. Ring B is first shifted along the wall by the registry $\tau/(2k-2)$ given by
the lock, so its cores need almost no rotation; ring A has natural tilts that wind around the ring ($150^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $30^\circ$, \dots\ at $k=6$), so every core must be
rotated. The bulk pair keeps its natural orientation. Validation: the unrelaxed one-constant energy difference at $\tau=0$ is $+183.7\,\kT$ against $+184.0\,\kT$ from the
image construction.

\paragraph{Energies} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_energy}; ranges over elasticity, core size and pair position; $k=6$ first, $k=4$--8 in brackets):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Ejection without flow}, natural $2k$ ring to untilted B: $+180$ to $+200\,\kT$ ($+155$ to $+200$). The passive state is stable.
\item \emph{Forcing a common tilt on the $2k$ ring}, from its natural winding tilts: $+375$ to $+475\,\kT$ ($+200$ to $+510$), nearly independent of $\tau$. This is the frustration.
\item \emph{Tilting the ejected structure} costs essentially nothing: $-38$ to $+3\,\kT$ between $\tau=0$ and $90^\circ$.
\item \emph{Under a common tilt} the ejected structure is cheaper than the forced $2k$ ring by 175--290\,$\kT$ (20--330); the sign is robust to elasticity, core size and pair position.
\end{itemize}
Forcing a common tilt therefore costs about twice what ejection costs, and the ejected structure, the largest ring that can tilt as a whole plus a bulk $+1$, wins as soon as the
flow imposes a common tilt.

\paragraph{Negative winding} ($k=-6$, same construction with $-\half$; Sec.~\ref{si:mirror}). The commensurate ring has 14 defects, whose natural tilts are uniform
($\pm60^\circ$, one arm into the bulk), while the 12-ring's tilts wind (50, 30, 10, \dots$^\circ$). Without flow, gaining two $-\half$ (14-ring plus a bulk $+1$, which requires
a new $\pm\half$ pair) costs $+64$ to $+181\,\kT$ plus four core energies, so passive layers keep 12. Under a common tilt the 12-ring costs $+350$ to $+480\,\kT$, the 14-ring
tilts by registry almost free, and the 14-ring with a bulk $+1$ is cheaper by 196--392\,$\kT$ minus the four core energies.

\section{Continuum models}\label{si:continuum}
\subsection{Passive Q-tensor with the measured flow}
We solve two-dimensional Beris--Edwards dynamics for $Q=S(\mathbf{nn}-\mathbb I/2)$ in the anchored disk,
\begin{equation}
\partial_tQ+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla Q=\lambda_c\mathbf E+(\boldsymbol\Omega Q-Q\boldsymbol\Omega)+\Gamma\mathbf H,\qquad \mathbf H=L\nabla^2Q+aQ-c\,\mathrm{tr}(Q^2)\,Q,
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf E$ and $\boldsymbol\Omega$ the strain-rate and vorticity tensors and one elastic constant. Units $L=a=\Gamma=1$ (core size about one cell), $S_\mathrm{eq}=0.876$ as in
MPCD, grid spacing 0.5, $R=31.83$, $k=6$; the anchoring $\theta=k\phi$ is held on a ghost ring $r\geq R$. In simple shear the director obeys
$\dot\theta=-\dot\gamma/2+(\lambda_c/2S)\dot\gamma\cos2\theta$, so $\lambda_c=\lambda S_\mathrm{eq}$ gives the MPCD Leslie angle, $\cos2\theta_L=1/\lambda$. The flow is imposed
(no backflow), $v_\theta(r)=U f(r)$, with $f$ the azimuthal profile measured in a passive MPCD swirl run (normalised to a maximum of 1, so $U$ is the peak speed). Runs start
from the passive ring of 12, relaxed without flow.

Without any fitting the model reproduces the passive MPCD results (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_qtensor}):
\begin{itemize}
\item above a flow threshold the ring ejects two defects (wall 10, bulk $+1$); below it the tilt coherence rises gradually (0.07 to 0.56) and jumps to 1.00 at ejection, as in MPCD
(0.2--0.5, then 0.9--0.96); $\lambda=1.2$ ejects only at the strongest flow, $\lambda=0.5$ never;
\item after ejection the tilt is 65--67$^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$ over a sevenfold range of $U$, 78--79$^\circ$ at $\lambda=4$ and 46$^\circ$ at $\lambda=1.2$; the bulk director sits at
the Leslie angle (29.1, 36.7, 16.5$^\circ$ against 30, 37.8, 16.8$^\circ$).
\end{itemize}
Changing only the flow profile isolates the role of the no-slip shear. Shear confined to the band next to the wall (rigid rotation inside) gives the same tilt (67.7 against
$66.7^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$; 79 against $78^\circ$ at $\lambda=4$). Removing the shear in that band (rigid rotation there, i.e.\ a slipping wall) gives 96--101$^\circ$ and a higher
ejection threshold. The angle is set by the shear next to the wall.

\subsection{Active Q-tensor with Stokes backflow}
The same Q dynamics with the flow solved from Stokes' equation with no slip, $\eta\nabla^4\psi=\partial_xf_y-\partial_yf_x$, $\mathbf v=(\partial_y\psi,-\partial_x\psi)$,
$\mathbf f=\nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, $\boldsymbol\sigma=-\zeta Q-\lambda_c\mathbf H+(Q\mathbf H-\mathbf HQ)$ (extensile for $\zeta>0$, backflow included), $\eta=1$
(rotational viscosity $\gamma_1=2S^2/\Gamma=1.53$, $K=2LS^2=1.535$, active length $\sqrt{K/\zeta}$). Validation: a uniform azimuthal force in the disk gives the analytic
$v_\theta=fr(R-r)/3\eta$. No flow is imposed.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Disk} ($k=6$, started from the passive ring of 12): at $\zeta=0.1$ a single vortex forms (circulation $\langle v_\phi\rangle/v_\mathrm{rms}=0.92$) but the ring keeps 12
(tilt coherence 0.47); at $\zeta=0.3$ and 1.0 the vortex ejects two defects (wall 10, bulk $+1$) and the layer tilts coherently by 61.7--62.1$^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$, independent
of the activity; 76.5$^\circ$ at $\lambda=4$, 37.1$^\circ$ at $\lambda=1.2$. The chain activity $\to$ vortex $\to$ ejection $\to$ $2k-2$ wall defects and a bulk $+1$ $\to$
flow-aligned tilt therefore appears in a continuum active nematic, not only in MPCD.
\item \emph{Ellipses} of the circle's area with arclength-uniform anchoring ($k=6$, $\lambda=2$; Fig.~\ref{fig:s_contell}): circle-like for $\varepsilon\leq1.2$ (10 wall defects,
bulk $+1$, coherent tilt 61--62$^\circ$); above $\varepsilon\approx1.4$--1.6 the single vortex breaks into two, the tilt coherence drops to $\approx0.5$ and the wall loses defects
(1--1.6 vacancies at $\zeta=0.3$, up to 4 at $\zeta=1$, $\varepsilon=2.5$). The transition moves to smaller aspect at stronger activity; at $\zeta=1$ the coherence curve nearly
coincides with MPCD. With activity only in a 4-cell band at the wall, an aspect-2 ellipse keeps a single vortex and 10.7 wall defects, as in MPCD: the elongation
instability is a bulk-activity effect.
\item \emph{Activity regimes} ($k=4$ and 16, $R=31.83$): laminar with static bulk defects up to $\zeta=3$; occasional bulk pairs from $\zeta=4$ and sustained pair creation from
$\zeta=7$--8, while both disks keep a single vortex and an ordered bulk spiral (order 1.1--1.3), except the sparse disk at $\zeta=8$ (order 0.19); at $\zeta=10$ the crowded
disk loses its vortex. The tilt and its crowding dependence in these
runs are discussed in Sec.~\ref{si:tilt}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Flat-wall theory}
The local limit of the cavity: a wall at $y=0$ in simple shear $v_x=sy$ (no slip), periodic along the wall with one site spacing $a_s$, anchoring $\theta(x,0)=\pi/2+\kappa x$
with $\kappa=\pi/a_s$, screened by one $+\half$ per period at depth $d$. With $\theta$ the director angle from the wall and one Frank constant $K$, Ericksen--Leslie gives
\begin{equation}
\gamma_1(\partial_t+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\theta=K\nabla^2\theta+\gamma_1\frac s2(\lambda\cos2\theta-1).
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The layer is a zero mode.} If $\theta_0(x,y)$ is a static solution with the defect at $x=0$, so is $\theta_0(x-D,y)+\kappa D$, with the same anchoring and energy.
Sliding the defect by $D$ rotates the far field by $\kappa D$ and the defect by $2\kappa D$: this is the lock (Sec.~\ref{si:lock}), now derived.
\item \emph{Leading order.} Beyond the screening length $a_s/2\pi$ the texture is uniform along the wall and the only stable state is the Leslie angle. If the near-wall region passes
on no torque, the layer slides along its zero mode until the far field is aligned:
\begin{equation}\tau_0=2\theta_L=\arccos(1/\lambda),\end{equation}
independent of $s$, $K$ and $\gamma_1$; only the ratio of strain to vorticity enters, and the no-slip wall fixes it.
\item \emph{Boundary-layer correction.} Linearising about $\theta_L$ gives the alignment length $\ell_E=\sqrt{K/(\gamma_1s\sqrt{\lambda^2-1})}$. A torque $\Gamma$ per unit length from
the band between wall and defects over-rotates the layer by $\Gamma\ell_E/K$; with the ansatz $\Gamma=\gamma_1sd/2$ (in the band the anchoring holds the director, so only the
vorticity acts)
\begin{equation}
\tau=\arccos\frac1\lambda+d\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_1s}K}\,(\lambda^2-1)^{-1/4}.\label{eq:s_tiltcorr}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Flat-wall Q-tensor simulations confirm each step (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_flatwall}): the far field aligns at $\theta_L$ within $1^\circ$, the registry shift equals the tilt, the tilt tends to
$\arccos(1/\lambda)$ at weak shear, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:s_tiltcorr} matches 35 runs ($\lambda=1.1$--4, a hundredfold range of shear, $a_s=10$--40) with no free parameter (ratio 0.97,
correlation 0.98). Steps 1--2 and the form of step 3 are derived; the prefactor is an ansatz. In these flat-wall runs $d/\ell_E=0.05$--0.29, while MPCD cores sit at
$d/\ell_E=0.5$--1.2; at strong shear the continuum core is pushed onto the first grid row and absorbed into the wall, so the flat-wall model cannot reach the MPCD regime
(Sec.~\ref{si:tilt}).

\section{The tilt in MPCD: mechanism and residuals}\label{si:tilt}
\subsection{Sparse layers: the flow-aligned tilt}
\paragraph{Mechanism.} MPCD rotates each particle by Jeffery's equation with Bretherton parameter $\lambda$ (the shear susceptibility scales the rate, not the angle), so the
flat-wall argument (Sec.~\ref{si:continuum}) applies directly. In passive circles under an imposed vortex the director at the defects' depth is rotated by 26--33$^\circ$
(Leslie angle $\theta_L=30^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$), and the tilt equals twice that rotation (65.5 against 63.7$^\circ$, 61.6 against 61.0$^\circ$).

\paragraph{Across $\lambda$} (active circles, area 2500, $\zeta=0.07$, no imposed flow; Fig.~\ref{fig:s_tiltlaw}). For $\lambda\geq2$ the overcharged state holds exactly
(wall $2k-2$, bulk $+1$, tilt coherence 0.96--0.99, the circulation never reverses) and the tilt follows $\arccos(1/\lambda)$ within $3^\circ$: 63.0, 71.7, 74.8 and $77.9^\circ$ at
$\lambda=2$, 3, 4, 6 against 60.0, 70.5, 75.5, $80.4^\circ$ ($k=6$, 3 seeds); 71.6 and $78.1^\circ$ at $\lambda=3$ and 6 for $k=4$; 70.0 and $77.5^\circ$ for $k=8$ (2 seeds each).
Below $\lambda\approx2$ the state breaks down: at $k=6$, $\lambda=1.5$ gives 8.9 wall defects (bulk $+1.6$, coherence 0.75), $\lambda=1.2$ gives 8.2 ($+1.9$, 0.61) and the tumbling
$\lambda=0.8$ 7.5 ($+2.3$, 0.42, reversing circulation); $k=4$ and 8 behave alike at $\lambda=1.5$. Four more seeds with fine output show a smooth crossover: wall 9.94, 9.60, 8.65 at
$\lambda=2$, 1.75, 1.5 (coherence 0.96, 0.90, 0.73), with depleting-escape rates of 0.03, 0.21 and 0.39 per wall defect per $10^3$\,t.u.

\paragraph{The threshold depends on size} (circles $R_\mathrm{eq}=0.5$--$2\,\la$, $k=6$, 5 seeds; wall $+\half$ of 10, bulk charge, coherence $C$):
\begin{center}\small\begin{tabular}{lcccc}\hline
$R_\mathrm{eq}/\la$ & $\lambda=1.5$ & $\lambda=1.75$ & $\lambda=2$ & $\lambda=3$\\\hline
0.5 & 11.9, $+0.04$, 0.35 & 11.6, $+0.14$, 0.41 & 11.3, $+0.31$, 0.52 & 10.0, $+0.97$, 0.90\\
0.75 & 10.9, $+0.55$, 0.63 & 10.6, $+0.72$, 0.72 & 10.2, $+0.89$, 0.88 & 10.0, $+1.00$, 0.96\\
1.0 & 9.7, $+1.15$, 0.72 & 9.7, $+1.16$, 0.75 & 10.1, $+0.96$, 0.95 & --\\
1.5 & 8.8, $+1.62$, 0.86 & 9.9, $+1.06$, 0.95 & 9.9, $+1.03$, 0.97 & --\\
2.0 & 9.0, $+1.48$, 0.94 & 9.6, $+1.20$, 0.97 & 9.8, $+1.09$, 0.98 & --\\\hline
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Small cavities under-eject: the ring stays close to the passive 12 because the flow cannot impose a common tilt (low $C$); stronger alignment rescues them ($R_\mathrm{eq}=0.5\,\la$ needs
$\lambda=3$). Large cavities over-eject: they stay coherent but lose wall defects at low $\lambda$, consistent with the jet route ($\cos\tau=1/\lambda$ points more of each jet inward) and
their stronger flow. The overcharged state lives in a window in $(\lambda,R/\la)$, its lower edge set by coherence and its upper edge by the leak.

\paragraph{Residuals.} The residual of the tilt against $\arccos(1/\lambda)$ falls from $+3.0^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$ through $+1.2$ and $-0.7$ to $-2.5^\circ$ at $\lambda=6$ ($k=6$).
Three candidate corrections: (i) the boundary-layer term, Eq.~\eqref{eq:s_tiltcorr}, with the measured $\gamma_1\approx60$ would add $\approx16^\circ$ at $\lambda=2$ and is always
positive, so it is absent in MPCD as written; (ii) order-dependent alignment: averaging Jeffery's equation over a cell gives $\lambda_\mathrm{eff}=\lambda(1+S_4)/(2S_2)$, and the
bulk Leslie angle measured in a sheared channel is 32.8, 29.9, $31.1^\circ$ for $U=5$, 10, $20\,\kT$, so $2\theta_L$ differs from $\arccos(1/\lambda)$ by up to $\pm3^\circ$ (at our
$U=10$ it is the bare value), while the cavity tilt barely changes with $U$ or density (61.5--64.8$^\circ$); (iii) splay $\neq$ bend adds $+2$ to $+3^\circ$ at both $\lambda=2$ and 4 in
the continuum disk. These set the scale of the residual but not its trend with $\lambda$, which remains unexplained.

\subsection{Crowded layers tilt less}
\paragraph{Observation} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_crowdobs}). Across the circle sweep ($\lambda=2$) the coherent-layer tilt falls from $64.6^\circ$ at wall density $\rho=k/\pi R\approx0.04$ to
$44.2^\circ$ at 0.14, and it collapses on the wall-defect spacing $a=2\pi R/(2k-2)$ ($1.7^\circ$ rms against a raw $7^\circ$; $k$ or $R$ alone leave 5--6$^\circ$): $\approx64^\circ$ for
$a\gtrsim20$ cells, $60^\circ$ at $a\approx15$, $\approx42^\circ$ at 7. Ellipses and ovals show the same fall in their local tilt, and passive layers under an imposed vortex fall as
steeply ($69\to54^\circ$ between $a=20$ and 7.7), so activity is not needed. $\arccos(1/\lambda)$ is therefore the limit reached by sparse layers.

\paragraph{What it is not.} Not a measurement effect: the tracker angle is the direction of $\nabla\!\cdot Q$, checked on seeded and real frames, and the drop survives in fully
coherent frames. Not a weaker mean shear: band shear and circulation do not depend on $k$ at fixed $R$, and passive layers drop without the defects' own counter-flow. Not weak
anchoring: the wall-cell director deviates by only 2.4--5$^\circ$ between defects at every spacing; what falls is the order at the wall ($S=0.91\to0.79$ between defects, $0.87\to0.65$ at
the cores; bulk 0.86--0.87). Not the finite-shear correction, which is positive and explains only the fall from 64--69$^\circ$ towards $60^\circ$. Not thermal noise: in MPCD at twice the
density (noise amplitude down by $\sqrt2$, 2 seeds) the crowded tilt of coherent frames is unchanged ($k=16$, $R=1.43\,\la$: $50.4^\circ$ at both densities), although the layer is
coherent less often (27\% of frames against 74\%) and the flow is faster.

\paragraph{What orders it} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_crowddepth}). Crowded cores sit closer to the wall (median depth $1.7\to1.2$ cells active, $1.9\to1.3$ passive, from sparse to crowded), and
the tilt is linear in $d\sqrt{s}\propto d/\ell_E$, the depth in units of the alignment length: $\tau\approx20^\circ+107\,d\sqrt{s}$ (passive, $2.3^\circ$ rms) and $24^\circ+59\,d\sqrt{s}$
(active, $3.0^\circ$ rms). Fitted on the passive crowding runs, this line predicts an independent shear scan at fixed spacing ($k=6$: $54.6\to66.9^\circ$ as the forcing grows; $k=13$,
with shallower cores, 12--15$^\circ$ lower at the same wall shear) within $\approx3^\circ$, until the tilt saturates at the strongest forcing. Depth is itself a response, so this orders
the data rather than explaining it. The drop also vanishes at large $\lambda$: sparse ($k=6$) and crowded ($k=14$) coherent-layer tilts in circles of area 2500 are $62.5/47.6^\circ$
($\lambda=2$), $71.9/65.7^\circ$ (3), $74.9/71.2^\circ$ (4) and $78.0/77.4^\circ$ (6), with the full layer and bulk $+1$ throughout.

\paragraph{A torque balance on the registry.} The flow drives the registry towards the Leslie angle and an elastic pull drives it back. Site pinning is settled
(Sec.~\ref{si:lock}); what is open is the restoring torque against the flow, which the static registry stiffness cannot supply because it weakens with crowding. Two observations support
an elastic pull towards the untilted registry: continuum disks reproduce a drop below $60^\circ$ only when their elastic pull points there ($K_1/K_3=0.55$: $63.6\to54.7^\circ$; one
constant or $K_1/K_3=1.82$: no drop), and the static tilt potential of the measured layers changes the sign of its first harmonic with crowding when the bulk is split or disordered, as
in MPCD. MPCD has $K_1\approx42>K_3\approx23\,\kT$, which at a flat wall favours the opposite sign, so the pull must come from the circle and its bulk. Crowded cores also twist back
towards the normal by $\approx5^\circ$ relative to the lock. The static potential is too weak and too fragile to make the balance quantitative, and the flow torque per defect is not
known.

\paragraph{MPCD and continuum regimes.} The continuum active disk at moderate activity is laminar, with static bulk defects and a coherent tilted spiral down to the centre, and shows
no crowding drop. MPCD has continual pair creation and wandering bulk defects: its interior has no coherent spiral (orientational order $\le0.2$ for $r<0.6R$, with normal local
$S\approx0.86$), only an ordered ring 5--10 cells inside the layer, whose lean (about $0.3\tau$) follows the tilt seed by seed; inside that ring the director peaks at 22--28$^\circ$ from
the tangent and never reaches $\theta_L$. A possible mechanism is that the disordered bulk cuts the elastic link between the layer and a flow-aligned bulk vortex. In the continuum,
noise that disorders the spiral (order 0.71--0.75) produces no drop, whereas stronger activity does: $7.9^\circ$ at $\zeta=3$ (laminar, ordered spiral), $7.7$ and $8.1^\circ$ at $\zeta=4$, 5,
then $9.4$, $10.2$, $10.7^\circ$ at $\zeta=6$--8, with both disks keeping a single vortex. These cores sit at $d/\ell_E\approx1.2$--1.7 (sparse) and 0.42--0.53 (crowded,
resolution-limited), bracketing the MPCD range 0.5--1.2: the stronger shear brings the continuum into the regime where crowding matters, so the drop needs no turbulence. The scan
cannot isolate a turbulent contribution (the largest step comes before sustained pair creation, and the wall shear grows with $\zeta$), and the continuum drop reaches about half of
MPCD's $14^\circ$ at $\zeta=3$--5 and about three-quarters at $\zeta=8$. The mechanism of the crowding drop remains open.

\section{Where the flow comes from}\label{si:hydro}
\paragraph{Sign convention.} The orientation in the defect output is the direction of $\nabla\!\cdot Q$, the tail of a $+\half$; bulk $+\half$ move against it (66--72\% of
steps), as expected for an extensile nematic.

\paragraph{The bulk drives, the layer brakes} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_stokes}). From the measured director and order parameter we build $Q=S(\mathbf{nn}-\mathbb I/2)$ and the active force
density $\mathbf f=-\nabla\!\cdot Q$, averaged over each flow-output interval (50\,t.u.; 40 intervals from the last 80\% of each run), split it into a wall band (within 4 cells of the
wall) and the bulk, and solve two-dimensional Stokes flow with no-slip walls for each part. The predicted flow is compared cell by cell with the measured interval-averaged flow (first
cell at the wall excluded); the unknown factor $\zeta_\mathrm{eff}/\eta$ is one positive coefficient per run, fitted to the full force. This explains 48--72\% of the variance of the
measured flow; the bulk part alone explains 47--73\%, and it reproduces the two-vortex component in ellipses (correlation 0.87--0.91). The wall band opposes the circulation: the wall
$+\half$ have their tails downstream and their heads pointing into the bulk and upstream. Point-defect hydrodynamics is not enough: bulk $+\half$ as point forces explain only 6--21\%, and a
particle model (Stokeslets plus logarithmic elasticity) neither keeps $2k-2$ defects at the wall nor makes a circulation; given the structure ($2k-2$ at the wall, $+1$ inside) it
reproduces circulation and tilt, but it cannot produce the structure.

\paragraph{The layer as an effective boundary} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_effbc}). Seen from the bulk, the layer of $2k-2$ defects converts the $k$-fold anchoring into an effectively planar
winding-1 boundary, $\theta=\phi+c$: exactly so in the one-constant limit, and smoothly from about 0.34 of the site spacing inside the defect ring, since the site pattern decays
as $e^{-2\pi\delta/\ell}$ or faster with the distance $\delta$ from the ring. A layer tilt $\tau$ turns this boundary into a spiral, $c=90^\circ+\tau/2$, whose extensile stress drives
the circulation that tilts the layer: a feedback through the elastic field rather than through forces at the wall. Its linear stability, with calibrated parameters and one assumed
core size, gives the onset of tilt and circulation at $R/\la\approx0.4$--0.6, where it is measured (Sec.~\ref{si:circles}). The bulk $+1$ is the charge enclosed by a winding-1
boundary. In the active cavity the bulk spiral is not elastic: flow alignment rotates the director in the wall layer by about the Leslie angle and erases the elastic spiral deeper in
the bulk (74--87$^\circ$ measured against $122^\circ$ for the elastic spiral; the interior has no coherent spiral, Sec.~\ref{si:tilt}); the azimuthal drive is confined to an annulus $r/R\approx0.6$--0.9 just
inside the layer.

\paragraph{The resident layer is not advected.} The wall layer as a whole moves at 0.002--0.004 of the local fluid speed (0.13--0.16): a tilted layer sits at a static, shifted registry.
A defect that is leaving does follow the flow (Sec.~\ref{si:escape}). Under an imposed reversal the layer is stick--slip (Sec.~\ref{si:swirl}).

\section{Elongated cavities}\label{si:shapes}
\paragraph{Shape sweep.} 675 active runs (3 seeds): circles, ellipses ($\varepsilon=1.2$--2.5, arclength-uniform anchoring) and Cassini ovals (aspect 1.12--2.46, polar-angle
arclength anchoring), at equivalent radii $R_\mathrm{eq}=1$, 1.5 and $2\,\la$ and imposed densities $\rho=0.04$--0.14, $10^4$\,t.u.\ each. The mirror sweep (Sec.~\ref{si:mirror})
repeats it with $k\to-k$.

\paragraph{One transition, set by the aspect ratio} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_phase}). Tilt coherence, circulation and the single-vortex state collapse together at aspect 1.25--1.37,
for ellipses and Cassini ovals alike and at every size. Below it the cavity is circle-like: at most 0.17 vacancies, bulk $\approx+1$, coherence 0.92--0.94, one vortex. Above it
the tilt coherence is $\approx0.45$, the two halves along the long axis counter-rotate 32--84\% of the time, and the layer has about 1, 3 and 4.5 vacancies at
$R_\mathrm{eq}=1$, 1.5 and $2\,\la$ (bulk charge 1.1, 1.8, 2.5). The local lock survives everywhere (0.91--0.97): what fails is the occupancy of the layer and the coherence of the
flow. A coherent flow alone restores the circle structure in a passive ellipse: under an imposed vortex a passive $\varepsilon=1.5$ ellipse goes from 12 to 10 wall defects with bulk
$+1$ and a coherent tilt (0.62--0.77), so curvature does not prevent a $2k-2$ layer (Sec.~\ref{si:swirl}); at $\varepsilon=2$ the ejection is partial (10--11).

\paragraph{How the layer is lost} (Figs.~\ref{fig:s_mech} and \ref{fig:s_restoring}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Escape-driven.} Per wall defect, escapes are 3--7 times more frequent in elongated cavities at every size. At the transition they start at the tips (aspect 1.2: tips 0.014
against flanks 0.003 per 20\,t.u.); by aspect $\geq2$ the flanks catch up. Across runs the vacancies follow the escape rate (correlation 0.88 circle-like, 0.62 elongated).
\item \emph{Refill is not starved.} A bulk $+\half$ reaches the wall band equally often in every shape (ratio 0.86--1.07), but a vacancy captures it about half as efficiently as in a
circle (net incorporation per vacancy 0.5--0.6 of the circle value).
\item \emph{The excess charge is free $+\half$.} Only 1--5\% of bulk $+\half$ have a $-\half$ within 4 cells, and the unpaired $+\half$ are spread uniformly along the long axis (mean
$|x|/A=0.39$--0.42). Elongated cavities have fewer bulk $-\half$ (0.1, 0.9, 2.4 against 0.4, 1.9, 3.9 at $R_\mathrm{eq}=1$, 1.5, $2\,\la$): less pair creation in their bulk.
\item \emph{No cascade.} A second escape right after the first is slightly less likely than chance (0.85--0.95 at 10--60\,t.u.), there is no excess at neighbouring sites (1.04--1.09),
the escape rate per wall defect does not rise as vacancies accumulate, and a tip escape does not raise escapes elsewhere. Circles show mild clustering (1.1--1.7 at lags below
100\,t.u.), consistent with ejection two at a time.
\item \emph{The restoring force collapses.} From the depth changes of 5.4 million wall-defect samples we build an effective force on the depth $d$ (mean depth change over 10\,t.u.\
divided by its variance, so the force is $-U'(d)$ in units of an effective temperature set by the active noise). The push away from the wall is the same in both classes, but the
pull back on a defect 2--3 cells deep drops from $-0.9$ to $-1.2$ (circle-like) to $-0.07$ to $-0.22$ (elongated), and the well depth from 1.6--2.5 to 0.36--0.73 (tips weakest, 0.42);
the noise is similar. This is the effective force, active jet and flow included; the elastic force alone is unchanged (Sec.~\ref{si:escape}).
\item \emph{Partial explanations and ruled-out causes.} Elongated-cavity defects sit slightly deeper (1.44 against 1.37 cells) and their self-propulsion is visible, but with
circle-like escape rates at the same depth and orientation these differences explain 21\% of the excess. Tilt misfit at curved sites (the lock holds at every site), static elasticity
of the layer (tip wells only $\approx8\%$ shallower against 25--35\% measured) and uneven site spacing (a circle with strongly modulated sites keeps its layer) are ruled out.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Bulk activity breaks the coherent state.} With no imposed flow, switching activity off everywhere except a 4-cell band at the wall keeps the single coherent vortex, the
coherent tilted layer and the full $2k-2$ wall even at aspect 2 (wall 10.6, coherence 0.85, no two-vortex intervals), where activity everywhere gives 7.1 and coherence 0.44. Activity
only in the bulk breaks the coherence and empties the layer even in a circle (7.9, coherence 0.51). The continuum model agrees (band-only activity at aspect 2: single vortex, 10.7 wall
defects). Near-wall activity sustains the coherent near-wall circulation that tilts and protects the layer, and the elongation instability is a bulk-activity effect.

\section{Mirror control: negative winding}\label{si:mirror}
Reversing the winding ($k\to-k$) puts $-\half$ defects in the wall layer; they are not self-propelled, so they test whether the leak of elongated cavities is
driven by the wall defects' own jets.
In the same 675 cavities as the $+k$ shape sweep (circles, ellipses, Cassini ovals; $R_\mathrm{eq}=1.0$, $1.5$, $2.0\,\la$; 3 seeds), the $-\half$ layer
holds where the $+\half$ layer leaks (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_mirror}). Medians over $k$, density and seeds, aspect $\geq1.4$: the $+\half$ layer loses 2.0 defects relative
to the circle (up to 4.7 at $R_\mathrm{eq}=2\,\la$) with an escape probability per wall defect of $0.017$ per 20\,t.u.; the $-\half$ layer loses $0.05$ with
$0.0006$ ($\approx30\times$ lower). In the largest elongated cavities the $-\half$ layer even gains (up to $0.9$ above the circle at $R_\mathrm{eq}=2\,\la$, aspect
2.5), and the bulk charge grows with it ($Q_b$ $0.8\to1.1$). The circulation collapses in both cases ($0.86\to0.09$ for $+k$, $0.66\to0.11$ for $-k$ at
$R_\mathrm{eq}=2\,\la$), so the loss of the coherent vortex alone does not empty the layer: the leak requires self-propelled wall defects, consistent with the
escape route of Sec.~\ref{si:shapes}. In circles the $-\half$ layer gains about 1.5 of the two defects predicted by the continuum (13.5 of 14 within 4 cells of the wall
at $R\approx1.25\,\la$ for $k=-6$, fewer in larger circles at the same $k$; 13.0 at aspect 2 from seeded starts, unique from 12 and 14), with bulk charge $+0.5$ to $+0.7$.
The gain is set by crowding: in the sweep circles it reaches the full 2 for wall densities $\rho\gtrsim0.09$ at $R_\mathrm{eq}\geq1.5\,\la$ ($1.8$--$2.1$; 2.0 at $R_\mathrm{eq}=2\,\la$), and is
$0.1$--$0.3$ for the sparsest walls at $R_\mathrm{eq}\geq1.5\,\la$. The shortfall at intermediate crowding relative to the continuum is open. Wall $-\half$ also keep a restoring force in active ellipses ($k=-6$, 2 seeds: $-1.8$ to $-2.1$ at 2.5--3 cells; passive, aspect 2: $-2.6$).
\paragraph{Lock and tilt of wall $-\half$.} For a wall defect of charge $s$ whose director field is $\theta=s\alpha+\theta_0$, the arm pointing at the nearest wall point
carries the wall director, so its tilt from the outward normal is $\tau=\psi_\mathrm{rel}/(1-s)$ modulo $\pi/(1-s)$, with $\psi_\mathrm{rel}$ the anchoring angle
relative to the normal. For $+\half$ this is the lock $\tau=2\psi_\mathrm{rel}$; for $-\half$ it is $3\tau=2\psi_\mathrm{rel}$ (mod $2\pi$; $\tau$ is defined modulo
$120^\circ$, $\tau=0$ being one arm into the wall). In all 675 mirror runs this is the best of 24 candidate relations, with concentration 0.96 and zero phase offset.
In a circle $\psi_\mathrm{rel}=(k-1)\phi$, so for either sign the ring that can take a common tilt by a uniform shift along the wall has $2|k-1|$ defects: $2k-2$ for
$+k$ (two fewer than $2|k|$) and $2|k|+2$ for $-k$ (two more). The wall then carries charge $k-1$ and the bulk $+1$ in both cases; the shift by one is set by the
rotation of the wall normal, not by the threefold symmetry of the $-\half$, which only rescales the tilt by $1/3$. The wall $-\half$ tilt coherently with the flow:
the sign of the common tilt follows the circulation in 94\% of flow frames in circles (+½: 100\%), with a mean signed tilt of $\approx10^\circ$ (+½: $59^\circ$) at
every wall density; per vortex half in elongated cavities 79\% and $9.5^\circ$. At rest (passive, 12 wall $-\half$, $k=-6$) the coherence is 0.1, i.e.\ no common tilt.

\paragraph{The gain needs a crowded wall.} The $-\half$ layer reaches the full $2|k|+2$, with an interior charge of $+1$, only for wall densities
$\rho=|k|/(\pi R_\mathrm{eq})\gtrsim0.09$ and $R_\mathrm{eq}\geq1.5\,\la$ (Table~\ref{tab:mirror_rho}); small cavities saturate near 1.3 extra defects, and sparse walls
barely gain (elongated sparse cavities even lose $-\half$). Positive winding, by contrast, holds exactly $2k-2$ for $k=2$--10 in circles. Counting wall-layer traffic
(stays of at least 10\,t.u.\ within 4 cells; arrivals from and departures to depths beyond 6 cells) separates supply from retention: sparse walls receive the
most $-\half$ (4--6 times the crowded rate at $R_\mathrm{eq}\geq1.5\,\la$), including pairs nucleating in the layer at a similar rate per unit length (60--65 vs 70--95 per
$10^3$ cells per $10^3$\,t.u.), but each stays only $\approx90$\,t.u.\ ($R_\mathrm{eq}\geq1.5\,\la$) and 56--58\% leave into the bulk; in crowded walls a $-\half$ stays $\approx1100$--1350\,t.u., most
are born in the layer and end there, by annihilation (38--58\% of exits) or as lost tracks (41--57\%). The shortfall of sparse walls is therefore one of retention, not of the cost of creating pairs. This agrees with the
pull-back of wall $-\half$, which grows strongly with crowding ($-1.7$ at $k=-6$ vs $-8.2$ at $k=-12$, depth 2--2.5 cells, same circle).
